# Mission

OEP exists to keep the hiring market running on **value transfer**, not **value
extraction** — and to do it in the open, where claims can be witnessed and verified.

This page states the argument plainly so you can attack it. If it's wrong, the right
move is a PR with evidence, not agreement in a thread.

## The argument

1. **AI as a black box that does existing work cheaper creates no net new value.**
   It reproduces what's already been produced — faster, with fewer people paid, and
   the savings routed to whoever owns the model. Same output, labor squeezed out. Run
   it to its end and there's no job left to support the next job: the base every job
   stands on gets consumed by the thing standing on it. This is arithmetic, not
   pessimism.

2. **The honest counter, conceded in full.** Every prior automation wave — the
   printing press, the PC, the internet — eventually created *more* work, not less.

3. **Why this time has a hidden dependency.** Those waves created more work only
   because the new tools were **open**: they lowered the barrier to creating value, so
   the displaced could re-enter as creators. AI's means of production — compute, data,
   models, distribution — are unusually **concentrated**. So the real claim isn't "AI
   kills jobs." It's that creative destruction depends on open tools, and we're about
   to run it on closed ones. That's the whole ballgame.

4. **The bet.** Use AI for value **transfer**: compound knowledge into innovation, make
   the individual sovereign and queryable, keep capability distributed enough that
   people re-enter as builders instead of being filtered out by a black box.

## Why "open" is the mechanism, not a mood

A truth kept to yourself is only half a truth — it has no outside witness to validate
it. Publishing it, and letting humans and machines query it, is what makes it true.

This is why OEP is structured as a public, verifiable evidence graph rather than a
private database or a trust-me brand. The honesty *is* the architecture.

## What success looks like

- A candidate is a **node**, not a PDF in an abyss — queryable, sovereign, honest by
  construction.
- An employer exposes **verified claims** (actual stack, team size, remote policy), not
  marketing copy — and the engineer who needs a teammate can query a peer *before* HR
  is involved.
- Matching is routed **around any single captor**. No walled garden owns both sides of
  the conversation.
- Every claim on either side carries a **falsifiable edge** to something a stranger can
  check.

The protocol is the email of hiring: open, federated, any server, any client.
